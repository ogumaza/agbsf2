This release converts the sound effects of Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers. Enchanted Riverbank in *Donkey Kong Country 3* now converts as one pair of files above water and another under water. The notes of Nintendo R&D2's PSG voices now follow the PSG's volume frame by frame. agbsf2 converts Game Boy Advance music to MIDI and SoundFont. It supports Konami's, Rare's, Quintet's, Nintendo R&D2's, Brownie Brown's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers, MP2K and Krawall.

The program runs on 64-bit Windows 10 or later. It doesn't require any runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.gba`, `.gsflib` or `.minigsf` files on `agbsf2.exe`. You can also run it in a terminal. `agbsf2 --help` lists the options. Each file's output goes in a folder next to it.

On a Mac with Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. On macOS the build also makes `Agbsf2.app`. You can drop files on it in the Finder.

## Changes since 1.6.5

### Quintet's driver

- The sound effects of the four *Super Robot Taisen* games are converted. They come after the music file's songs in the numbering. A sound effect plays as the driver's routine for sound effects starts it, without the music. Each of its channels plays on one of the driver's channels for sound effects, with the sound effects' samples and noise macros. A channel whose data starts with `FF` is left to the music. The MIDI file ends when the last of the sound effect's channels ends. A sound effect that plays only rests gets no files.

### Nintendo R&D2's driver

- The sound effects of *A Link to the Past*, *Super Mario Advance 2* and *Super Mario Advance 3* are converted. They come after the driver's sequences in the numbering. A sound effect is a track of one of the sound effects' sequences. The game plays it on a player other than the one that plays the music. Its MIDI file holds the track and each track that the track starts with `F8`. The file ends when the last of them ends.
- For each segment of a square's or the noise's envelope, the driver works out a whole volume from 0 to 15 for the segment's start and one for its end. The PSG's envelope then steps the volume from one to the other. A SoundFont envelope can't depend on the velocity and the volumes. The square notes used to fall too soon after their start and then hold too loud. A square's or the noise's note now plays at full velocity. Its track's CC11 follows the channel's volume, frame by frame. The note lasts until the channel falls silent or another note takes the channel. The zones of the squares and the noise play at a constant level.
- The wave channel has five levels: none, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%. At the start of each segment of the wave's envelope, the driver picks one of them from the velocity and the volumes. The MIDI file used to play the level before the driver picked the step. The wave's notes now play at full velocity too. Their CC11 follows the level that the driver picks. A released wave voice steps down through the levels as it fades. The wave's zones no longer have a release.
- A PSG note panned to one side plays on that side alone. The driver doubles the note's level there, up to the channel's highest volume. The MIDI file now stops at that volume too. A loud note panned to one side used to play up to 6 dB louder than in the game.
- A sequence without a loop ends with its last track. A PSG channel can still be fading then. The MIDI file now ends where the last PSG channel falls silent. The ends of those fades used to be cut off.
- The driver plays velocities up to 255. A MIDI velocity stops at 127. Notes above 127 used to play at 127, up to 6 dB too quietly. On a track whose sample notes play above 127, each velocity v is now written as 127 × √(v/s). Here s is the track's loudest velocity. The track's CC11 raises its levels by s/127 to make up for it.

### Rare's driver

- *Donkey Kong Country 3* plays Enchanted Riverbank (tune 12) as two arrangements: channels 0-3 above water and channels 4-8 under water. The game's code holds the channels of the other arrangement at a level of 0. The mixer then leaves out their notes. Those notes don't count towards the limit of 8 voices either. The tune now gets two pairs of files: `game_12.mid` and `game_12.sf2` above water, and `game_12_underwater.mid` and `game_12_underwater.sf2` under water. Each pair leaves out the channels of the other arrangement. The conversion used to play both arrangements at once. The voice limit then cut out notes that the game plays.
- `--info` lists Enchanted Riverbank twice, with "(under water)" on the second line. A conversion's messages give the version's name after the song's number. Detection's report gives the channels of each arrangement.

## Known limitations

- The sound effects of Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers play on their own in their conversions. In the game, a sound effect of Quintet's driver takes each sound channel that it plays on from the music while it plays. A sound effect of Nintendo R&D2's driver and the music take voices from each other by their priorities.
- *Donkey Kong Country 3* fades Enchanted Riverbank from one arrangement to the other when the player dives in or climbs out. Each of the tune's files holds one arrangement from start to end. The game can also start a tune at a marker partway through. The conversion plays each tune from its start.
- With Nintendo R&D2's driver, the MIDI file changes a square's or the noise's CC11 at the start of a frame. The PSG's envelope steps the volume at 64 Hz. The hardware's step can then come up to a frame before the step in the MIDI file. At 25% and 50%, the game's wave plays a little quieter and coarser than the SoundFont's. Where a track's loudest velocity would take its CC11 above 127, the track's loudest notes play quieter than in the game.
- Apart from the sound effects of Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers and the channels of *Donkey Kong Country 3*, the limitations listed for 1.6.0 still apply. See "Accuracy" and "Known issues" in the README for the details.
