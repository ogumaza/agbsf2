In this release, the sample notes of Nintendo R&D2's driver fade as the game's envelopes do. This affects *Super Mario Advance 2: Super Mario World*, *Super Mario Advance 3: Yoshi's Island* and *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past*. agbsf2 converts Game Boy Advance music to MIDI and SoundFont. It supports Konami's, Rare's, Quintet's, Nintendo R&D2's, Brownie Brown's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers, MP2K and Krawall.

The program runs on 64-bit Windows 10 or later. It doesn't require any runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.gba`, `.gsflib` or `.minigsf` files on `agbsf2.exe`. You can also run it in a terminal. `agbsf2 --help` lists the options. Each file's output goes in a folder next to it.

On a Mac with Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. On macOS the build also makes `Agbsf2.app`. You can drop files on it in the Finder.

## Changes since 1.8.1

### Nintendo R&D2's driver

- The driver's envelopes move in straight lines in level. A SoundFont's decay falls in a straight line in decibels. The zones' attack, hold, decay and sustain could only approximate the driver's envelopes. A note that faded out in the game could play too loud during the fade and go on sounding after the game fell silent. Each sample zone now plays at a constant level until the release. The track's CC11 follows the envelopes of its sample voices frame by frame. In 1.8.1 only the notes in legato did that.
- A track's notes that sound together share CC11. CC11 then follows the mean of their envelopes, weighted by their power. The track plays as loud as in the game.
- In 1.8.1, a change of a track's volume or pan after the release of a note in legato set CC11 back to the track's full level. The release then played louder. CC11 now keeps the envelope's level that the voice had reached.
- The presets named `Bank B legato N` are gone. A track's notes in legato play on the instrument's preset.
- No zone holds attenuation for an envelope that peaks below the full level any more. The SoundFont specification says that a player applies all of a zone's attenuation. Such a player played these zones too quietly. It now plays them at the same level as other players.

## Known limitations

- With Nintendo R&D2's driver, a track's notes that sound together share CC11. Where they stand at different points of their envelopes, each note can play louder or quieter than in the game. That happens most where a note's release sounds under the track's next note.
- Apart from the envelopes of Nintendo R&D2's driver, the limitations listed for 1.8.1 still apply. See "Accuracy" and "Known issues" in the README for the details.
