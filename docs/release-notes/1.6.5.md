This release corrects levels and pans in the conversions of MP2K and of Rare's, Nintendo R&D2's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers. agbsf2 converts Game Boy Advance music to MIDI and SoundFont. It supports Konami's, Rare's, Quintet's, Nintendo R&D2's, Brownie Brown's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers, MP2K and Krawall.

The program runs on 64-bit Windows 10 or later. It doesn't require any runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.gba`, `.gsflib` or `.minigsf` files on `agbsf2.exe`. You can also run it in a terminal. `agbsf2 --help` lists the options. Each file's output goes in a folder next to it.

On a Mac with Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. On macOS the build also makes `Agbsf2.app`. You can drop files on it in the Finder.

## Changes since 1.6.4

### Every driver

- Each instrument's global zone holds a modulator from CC10 to the pan with an amount of 500. It takes the place of the default modulator. The SoundFont specification gives the default an amount of 1000. FluidSynth, BASSMIDI and most other SoundFont players use 500. A player that follows the specification now pans the notes as they do.
- The SoundFont specification requires a sample's loop to last 32 points or more. Some players can't play a shorter loop. The SoundFonts of Konami's, Quintet's, Brownie Brown's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers and Krawall now repeat a shorter loop until it's long enough. MP2K's, Rare's and Nintendo R&D2's SoundFonts did so already. A loop repeated that way plays the same points.

### MP2K

- The MIDI files give each side of a track the driver's level. The driver computes the two levels from the track's volume and pan. CC11 and CC10 together give those levels under the constant-power pan law of MIDI RP-036. Most SoundFont players use that law. CC7 stays at 127. The files used to hold the track's volume in CC7 and its pan in CC10. A player then set the two sides by its pan law. The driver sets them in a straight line from one side to the other.
- The PSG plays a note in the middle, or on one side alone where that side's level is at least twice the other's. While every note a track plays is a PSG note, the track's CC10 and CC11 follow that rule. Where the game's mixer is mono, CC10 stays at 64 on every track that the PSG's rule doesn't cover.
- The marked loop now also starts on the second pass where a player that loops on the markers would play a note more than 1 dB louder or quieter on either side than the next pass. The file then plays the first pass once before the marked loop.
- A velocity of v is written as 127 × √(v/127). A SoundFont player squares the velocity by default. The driver scales the level in a straight line. The SoundFonts no longer replace the default modulators.
- A sample note holds its full level for one frame between its attack and decay. The driver does the same. The SoundFont's decay used to start a frame early. A voice with a decay of 0 then fell to its sustain level at once.
- The PSG's zones get no reverb from CC91. On the GBA, the driver's reverb works on the mixer's output. The PSG doesn't pass through the mixer.
- Track 9 moves off channel 10, to the first free channel after it or else to the last free one before it. Players keep channel 10 for drums. A song that plays on all 16 channels keeps track 9 on channel 10. Its voices then get copies in bank 128. `--single-sf2` moved track 9 already.
- A sample drum's zone splits it between the sides in the driver's ratio. A sample drum panned hard to one side plays up to 3 dB quieter there than in the game. A PSG drum follows the PSG's rule on pans. On one side alone, its zone holds 7.5 dB of attenuation. FluidSynth, BASSMIDI and most other SoundFont players apply 0.4 of it. That takes off the 3 dB that the RP-036 pan law adds on that side. A player that follows the SoundFont specification plays the drum 4.5 dB too quietly there.

### Rare's driver

- Each channel that mutes a key starts with the General MIDI 2 Controller Destination Setting message. With the message, the pressure of a key scales the level of that key's notes on the channel. A player that follows the message, such as BASSMIDI, then silences the muted notes. BASSMIDI ignores the SoundFont's mute modulator.
- A velocity or volume (CC7) of v is written as 127 × √(v/127). A SoundFont player squares both by default. The driver scales the level in a straight line. BASSMIDI ignores a modulator from CC7. It used to play a track at CC7 64 about 6 dB too quietly.

### Nintendo R&D2's driver

- The squares and the noise span the same range as the wave. On the GBA, the three swing between the same two levels. The squares and the noise used to play 6 dB too quietly against the samples and the wave.
- The PSG plays a note in the middle at the centre pan, 0x40. At any other pan, it plays the note at twice the level on one side alone. On a track whose notes are all PSG notes other than drums, CC10 and CC11 follow that rule. The driver caps a note's doubled level at the PSG's highest level. The MIDI file doubles every such note. A loud one can then play up to 6 dB louder than in the game.
- Each drum's zone splits it between the sides in the driver's ratio. A drum panned hard to one side plays up to 3 dB quieter there than in the game.
- An envelope that peaks below the full level gives its zone the difference as attenuation. FluidSynth, BASSMIDI and most other SoundFont players apply only 0.4 of a zone's attenuation. The zone therefore holds 2.5 times the attenuation. A player that follows the SoundFont specification plays the zone too quietly: 6 dB down becomes 15 dB.

### Ubisoft Milan's driver

- Each track starts with CC7 and CC11 at 127, CC10 at 64, and CC91 and CC93 at 0. Players used to take CC7 at its default of 100. They then played the music about 4 dB too quietly. They also applied their default reverb and chorus.
- A drum's velocity of v is written as 127 × √(v/64). A square's starting volume of v is written as a velocity of 127 × √(v/15). The wave's volumes of 100%, 50% and 25% are written as 127, 90 and 64. A SoundFont player squares the velocity by default. The driver scales the level in a straight line.

### Notes and libraries

- The README's notes on Konami's, Quintet's and Brownie Brown's drivers correct what they say about the PSG's DC offset. In the SoundFont, the square waves have a DC offset at duty cycles other than 50%. The wave channel's patterns have none.
- The README says how BASSMIDI plays the output of `--psg-exclusive`. BASSMIDI ends a note of a class in about 4 ms. The PSG notes whose release is quicker than FluidSynth's fade get no class. In BASSMIDI, those notes fade out over their release where the game cuts them off.
- agbsf2 now builds on seqlib 1.3.0 and xsflib 1.5.0.
